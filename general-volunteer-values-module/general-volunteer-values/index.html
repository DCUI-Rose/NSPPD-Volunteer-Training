01

Purpose — Why This Matters

Every volunteer on the medical unit is a first responder and a representative of Christ at the same time. How you carry yourself, speak, and work with your team is as much a part of your service as any medical skill. This module covers the six areas of personal conduct expected of every volunteer, however you're deployed.

  • Serve with Joy and Humility
  • Compassionate Demeanor
  • Integrity and Honesty
  • Diligence and Excellence
  • Respect for All Persons
  • Honor Team Unity

Our motto

Healing hands, Christlike heart, gracious speech. Hold all three together — competence without character, or character without competence, both fall short of the standard.

02

Serve with Joy and Humility

Matthew 23:11 (KJV)

"He that is greatest among you shall be your servant."

  • Your service is unto God, not for recognition.
  • No task is too small — every role is a ministry.
  • Be dependable, punctual, and willing to serve joyfully.
03

Compassionate Demeanor

Ephesians 4:32 (KJV)

"Be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted…"

  • Maintain a warm and calm presence.
  • Be quick to listen, slow to speak, and gentle.
  • Treat everyone with the same care as your own loved ones.
04

Integrity and Honesty

2 Corinthians 8:21 (KJV)

"Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men."

  • Be truthful in all documentation and dialogue.
  • Own up to mistakes with humility and take responsibility.
  • Guard confidentiality carefully.
05

Diligence and Excellence

Ecclesiastes 9:10 (KJV)

"Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might…"

  • Be focused, attentive, and thorough in every duty.
  • Keep improving through learning and practice.
  • Remember: you serve as unto the Lord (Colossians 3:23–24).
06

Respect for All Persons

Matthew 25:40 (KJV)

"In as much as ye have done it unto one of the least of these… ye have done it unto me."

  • Be kind to all: elderly, children, the poor, disabled, or distressed.
  • Show no favouritism or bias.
  • Treat everyone's dignity as sacred, especially in vulnerable moments.
07

Honor Team Unity

Philippians 2:3 (KJV)

"Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory… let each esteem other better than themselves."

  • Support your team, and speak well of each other.
  • Avoid gossip, complaints, or comparisons.
  • Approach conflict with love, maturity, and a solution-minded spirit.
08

Manner of Speaking — Gracious and Uplifting Words

Colossians 4:6 (KJV)

"Let your speech be always with grace, seasoned with salt…"

  • Speak kindly, even under pressure.
  • Avoid sarcasm, anger, or criticism.
  • Uplift and encourage through every word.

Examples of gracious phrases

"You're in good hands; we're here for you." · "Let me explain this carefully so you feel informed and at peace." · "I understand this is hard — let's walk through it together." · "Your concerns matter; let's address them one by one."

09

Clear and Respectful Communication

Proverbs 17:27 (KJV)

"He that hath knowledge spareth his words…"

  • Use simple, respectful language.
  • Speak with a steady tone — never rushed, rude, or condescending.
  • Pause for understanding; encourage dialogue.
10

Spiritually-Aware Speech (When Appropriate)

Proverbs 25:11 (KJV)

"A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver."

  • Offer prayer or Scripture when appropriate and welcomed.
  • Let your words reflect the love, peace, and truth of Christ.
  • Be supportive, faith-building, and gentle — never forceful.
11

Teamwork — Unity of Purpose & Mutual Respect

"Two are better than one…" (Ecclesiastes 4:9). "That there should be no schism in the body… but that the members should have the same care one for another" (1 Corinthians 12:25).

1
Unity of Purpose

Keep the mission of service, healing, and love at the centre. Avoid division, competition, or isolation. Celebrate each other's efforts and work toward common goals.

2
Mutual Respect — Romans 12:10

"Be kindly affectioned… in honour preferring one another." Acknowledge every role, big or small. Build one another up in love. Give grace, not criticism.

12

Servant Leadership & Bearing One Another's Burdens

1
Servant Leadership — Matthew 23:11

Lead by doing, not demanding. Lift others, don't look down on them. Be willing to do what you ask of others.

2
Bear One Another's Burdens — Galatians 6:2

Help teammates who are overwhelmed or discouraged. Offer practical support and prayer. Show up with encouragement, not complaint.

13

Summary — The 3 E's of Conduct

1
Excellence

Be competent, careful, and consistent.

2
Empathy

See through others' eyes; act with compassion.

3
Edification

Speak and act in ways that heal, build, and bless.

14

Volunteer Commitment Declaration

My Commitment

  • As a volunteer, I commit to serve with a Christlike spirit — joyfully, humbly, and faithfully.
  • I will honor my teammates, treat those I serve with compassion and dignity, and represent Jesus in all I do.
  • I will strive for excellence, walk in unity, and speak with grace, so that God is glorified in every act of service.
15

Documenting a Medical Incident

Every guest interaction is someone's whole day. A clear, written record protects them, protects you, and keeps the whole medics chain working as one team.

  • Continuity of care — anyone who picks up the case afterward (Group Lead, Coordinator, stadium medical team) knows exactly what happened and what's already been done.
  • Accountability — a written, timestamped record shows the right steps were followed and the right people were informed.
  • Safeguarding — protects the guest, especially where next-of-kin, hospital transfer, or a vulnerable person is involved.
  • Learning for next time — patterns across incidents help the HOD improve training, staffing, and stadium liaison for future conferences.

Golden rule

One form per incident — even minor ones. If it needed a medical assessment, it needs a form. A quick check that turns out fine still gets logged; it protects you and the guest either way.

16

When, Who & The Form At a Glance

The attending Medic completes the form — the person who assessed the guest, not a passer-by. Fill it in at the time of the incident, while details are fresh, and submit it from your phone immediately, on the spot — don't batch incidents for later.

Seven sections, in the order you'll fill them:

1
Incident Details

Date, time, block, who's notified.

2
Person Details

Who the guest is, and their condition.

3
Presenting Signs

Medical red flags + spiritual context.

4
Assessment

Observe · Approach · Responsiveness · ABCDE.

5
Actions & Escalation

What you did, and who you told.

6
Outcome

How it resolved.

7
Sign-off

Medic, Group Lead, Coordinator.

17

Quick Reference — Before You Go Out

  • Serve with joy, humility, and dependability.
  • Speak with grace, patience, and respect — every time.
  • Treat every guest, volunteer, and teammate with equal dignity.
  • Protect team unity — no gossip, comparison, or division.
  • Medical assessment always comes first.
  • One incident form, every time, filled in on site.
  • Name who you escalated to — every time.

Healing Hands, Christlike Heart, Gracious Speech

  • Excellence. Empathy. Edification.
  • Your character is as much a part of your service as your skill.
  • Ask your Coordinator if you're ever unsure.
